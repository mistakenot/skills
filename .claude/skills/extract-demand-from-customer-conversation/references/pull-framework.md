# The PULL Framework (Rob Snyder)

Source: Rob Snyder, "The PULL Quickstart Guide," *The Physics of Startups*.
<https://thephysicsofstartups.substack.com/p/the-pull-quickstart-guide>

PULL is a model for **pre-product-market-fit demand**. It reframes "why does
someone buy?" away from pain points and value propositions toward *context and
alternatives*. The central image: a customer with real pull will **rip the
product out of your hands** — no persuasion required.

## Pull vs. push

- **Pull** — customers actively seek out and pursue your solution. They spend
  their own energy to buy. Short cycles, little follow-up needed.
- **Push** — sellers convince reluctant buyers. Long cycles, heavy follow-up,
  playbooks and scripts doing the work. Persistent push is the signature of
  **Not(PMF)**.

> "Pain points, problems, value, and wanting your product are NOT RELEVANT."
> What determines a purchase is a customer's contextual priorities and their
> available alternatives.

## The four conditions (the acronym)

A person buys only when **all four** hold **simultaneously**:

| Letter | Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | **Project** | A specific, *product-agnostic* task on their priority list — e.g. "organize my sales data," not "buy a CRM." |
| **U** | **Unavoidable** | The project is their #1 current priority right now; they cannot defer it. |
| **L** | **List** | They have considered a list of the available options for accomplishing the project. |
| **L** | **Limitations** | Every existing option falls short — the limitations block them from completing the project. |

**The full statement:** "A person will rip the product out of your hands if she
has PULL: she has a **Project** on her to-do list, that is **Unavoidable** right
now… she considers a **List** of available options… but her existing options
don't get her there — they have **Limitations**."

## Diagnostic questions

Sanity checks to validate whether real PULL exists:

1. **Unavoidable check:** Is it acceptable if this person *postpones* this project?
   (If yes → not Unavoidable → no pull.)
2. **Limitations check:** Assuming they pursue the project, would they be
   satisfied using an *alternative* method? (If yes → no real Limitation → no pull.)

**Hail-Mary question when uncertain:** "What would you pay me to solve for you?"

## Signals you're in Not(PMF)

- Customers need significant persuasion to buy.
- Sales cycles are long and require extensive follow-up.
- Standard tactics (sales playbooks, outbound scripts, PLG) aren't producing
  results comparable to what they produce for competitors with real demand.
- You generate some revenue but growth stays hard.

## Implementation hierarchy (most → least important)

Debug **upstream** first. Most problems are solved at the ICP level, not with
downstream tactics.

1. **ICP = Destiny.** Define the Ideal Customer Profile as: *"Who has PULL right
   now?"* Anchor on one real customer who buys with minimal convincing, tolerates
   an early/imperfect version, and pushes for improvements rather than churning.
2. **Product.** Must let customers overcome their **Limitations** while
   accomplishing their **Project**. Further requirements surface through sales
   objections and delivery friction.
3. **Sales vibe.** With real pull, customers spend energy to buy. If you don't
   see that energy, pull probably isn't there.
4. **Getting people to talk.** *Before* finding pull: use personal monopolies and
   interesting angles. *After*: use creative "toll booths" — reach customers
   inside their pull situation in ways that only make sense given their context.
5. **Sales discovery.** Aim to *fill out their PULL*, not surface pain points.
   Flow: "Thanks for meeting — why'd you take the call? What would help?" → set
   agenda → "Mind if I ask questions to understand your context?" → surface their
   PULL → play it back to confirm.
6. **Pitch & demo.**
   - **Supply statement (60–90s):** "We are [category] enabling [ICP] to do
     [project] without [limitations]."
   - **Describe the unblocked life:** "Instead of XYZ, you now do ABC and get DEF."
   - **Fit check:** ask whether this conceptually matches their needs.
   - **Advance:** "What do you need to know to bring this into your organization?"
   - **Demos:** optional, ≤3 minutes, minimal screens — no setup/settings pages.
7. **Deal strategy.** Optimize for **velocity (pace of learning)**, not maximized
   price. Start small to reduce approval complexity. Price early just enough to
   confirm real pull (they won't ghost if it fails) without artificially slowing
   deals. Services revenue early can preserve cash.
8. **Sales process.**
   - *Phase 1 — Fit:* confirm PULL alignment and org readiness (1–2 calls).
   - *Phase 2 — Project management:* work backward from a completion date;
     find the minimum steps to purchase; keep the person-with-pull as champion.
   - *Group demos:* champion introduces; seller reinforces the demand/supply
     overview and supports the champion in peer meetings.
9. **Post-sales.** True PMF needs consistent retention/expansion. Find the
   "something" that makes customers *not* churn (often specific and
   counterintuitive), find its leading indicator, then engineer onboarding so
   every new customer reaches that indicator.

## Common mistakes

1. **Debugging at the wrong level** — fixing downstream tactics (manufacturing
   urgency) instead of upstream ICP definition.
2. **Confusing organizational with individual pull** — *companies* don't have
   pull, *individuals* do. A senior employee often has clearer pull and real
   authority than an enthusiastic junior.
3. **Too few conversations** — target 5–15+ per week. If 3–5 calls with
   prospects matching your PULL hypothesis show no pulling behavior, revise the
   hypothesis.
4. **Over-relying on research** — *doing the job* with potential customers
   reveals pull better than interviews alone.
5. **Variable deal velocity** — a sign of incomplete PULL understanding, where
   velocity = [time-to-pull] + [sales-cycle-length].

## The one thing

> "Everything else about startups is downstream of this. If you don't find PULL,
> nothing else matters."

One "Hell Yes" customer becomes the replicable model. The startup's fundamental
job is to systematize that single successful case, over and over.
